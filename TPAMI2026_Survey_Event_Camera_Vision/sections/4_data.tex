\section{Datasets \& Evaluations}
\label{sec:data}

The rapid growth of event-based vision research has been accompanied by an expanding ecosystem of datasets, benchmarks, and simulators. In this section, we provide a structured overview of the resources that underpin the methods reviewed in \cref{sec:method}, organized by task domain. We also discuss evaluation metrics and identify gaps in current benchmarking practices.

\subsection{Datasets by Task}
\label{sec:data_bytask}

We catalog over 60 datasets organized by their primary application domain. Many datasets serve multiple tasks (\eg, DSEC~\cite{gehrig2021dsec} supports stereo depth, optical flow, segmentation, and SLAM); we list each under its most common usage.

\paragraph{Classification.} N-MNIST and N-Caltech101~\cite{orchard2015nmnist} were the earliest event classification datasets, created by recording static images with a saccading event camera. CIFAR10-DVS~\cite{li2017cifar10dvs} followed a similar protocol at 128$\times$128 resolution. N-Cars~\cite{sironi2018ncars} provided real-world binary classification. N-ImageNet~\cite{kim2021nimagenet} (ICCV 2021) scaled to 1,000 classes with 1.78M samples, establishing the primary benchmark for event-based recognition. ES-ImageNet~\cite{bi2021esimagenet} provided a million-sample synthetic alternative.

\paragraph{Object Detection.} The Prophesee Gen1 dataset~\cite{perot2020red} (304$\times$240, 39h of driving, 255K boxes) and the 1Mpx dataset~\cite{perot2020red} (1280$\times$720, 14h, 25M boxes) are the standard benchmarks. PKU-DAVIS-SOD~\cite{zhao2023sodformer} provides streaming detection annotations on DAVIS346. EvDET200K~\cite{wang2025mvheat} (CVPR 2025) offers 10 categories at HD resolution.

\paragraph{Semantic Segmentation.} DDD17-Seg~\cite{binas2017ddd17} provides 6-class driving segmentation at 346$\times$260. DSEC-Semantic~\cite{sun2022ess} extends DSEC with 11-class pixel-level labels at 640$\times$440. EventScape~\cite{gehrig2021ramnet} offers synthetic labels from CARLA.

\paragraph{Object Tracking.} Dedicated tracking datasets have grown rapidly: FE108~\cite{zhang2021fe108} (108 sequences), VisEvent~\cite{wang2023visevent} (820 pairs), COESOT~\cite{tang2022ceutrack} (1,354 sequences, 90 categories), EventVOT~\cite{wang2024hdetrack} (CVPR 2024, 1,141 HD videos), FELT~\cite{wang2024felt} (1,044 long-term videos), and CRSOT~\cite{tang2024crsot} (cross-resolution).

\paragraph{Optical Flow.} MVSEC~\cite{zhu2018mvsec} and DSEC-Flow~\cite{gehrig2021dsec} provide real-world flow benchmarks. BlinkFlow~\cite{zhang2023blinkflow} (IROS 2023) uses synthetic data to push estimation limits.

\paragraph{Depth Estimation.} MVSEC~\cite{zhu2018mvsec} and DSEC~\cite{gehrig2021dsec} provide stereo depth ground truth from LiDAR. EDS~\cite{hidalgo2022eds} supports event-aided sparse odometry.

\paragraph{Action and Gesture Recognition.} DVS128 Gesture~\cite{amir2017dvs128gesture} (11 classes, 29 subjects) remains the standard gesture benchmark. HARDVS~\cite{wang2023hardvs} (300 classes), THU-EACT-50~\cite{gao2023thueact} (50 classes, multi-view), and DailyDVS-200~\cite{wang2024dailydvs200} (200 classes, 47 subjects) have scaled event-based action recognition.

\paragraph{Human Pose.} DHP19~\cite{calabrese2019dhp19} provides multi-view DVS data with Vicon motion capture ground truth. EHPT-XC (NeurIPS 2024) extends to extreme conditions.

\paragraph{Reconstruction.} 2D paired datasets include HQF~\cite{stoffregen2020hqf}, RLED~\cite{liu2024rled}, EvLight~\cite{liang2024evlight}, EventAID~\cite{cho2024eventaid}, and EventHDR~\cite{zou2025eventhdr}. 3D datasets include the IJRR dataset~\cite{mueggler2017ijrr}, CED~\cite{scheerlinck2019ced}, TUM-VIE~\cite{klenk2021tumvie}, VECtor~\cite{gao2022vector}, and PAEv3d~\cite{wang2024paev3d}.

\paragraph{SLAM and Visual Odometry.} The IJRR dataset~\cite{mueggler2017ijrr}, MVSEC~\cite{zhu2018mvsec}, DSEC~\cite{gehrig2021dsec}, TUM-VIE~\cite{klenk2021tumvie}, VECtor~\cite{gao2022vector}, M3ED~\cite{chaney2023m3ed}, and EVIMO2~\cite{burner2022evimo2} provide multi-sensor data for event-based localization and mapping.

\paragraph{Language and Understanding.} This is the newest dataset category, emerging in 2025. Talk2Event~\cite{zheng2025talk2event} provides 30K+ referring expressions for event grounding. N-ImageNet-Chat and Event-Chat~\cite{zhou2025eventgpt} supply event-language pairs for MLLM training. EventVL-Base~\cite{li2025eventvl} (1.4M pairs) and EventMind~\cite{kong2026eventflash} (500K+ instructions) support event MLLM development.

\subsection{Simulators}
\label{sec:data_simulators}

Event camera simulators are essential for generating large-scale training data and evaluating algorithms under controlled conditions. ESIM~\cite{rebecq2018esim} (CoRL 2018) provides photorealistic event simulation via adaptive rendering coupled with UnrealCV. rpg\_vid2e~\cite{gehrig2020vid2e} (CVPR 2020) recycles existing video datasets by driving ESIM from interpolated video frames. v2e~\cite{hu2021v2e} (CVPRW 2021) generates realistic DVS events from video with detailed noise modeling including pixel-level threshold mismatch, bandwidth limitations, and leak events. DVS-Voltmeter~\cite{lin2022dvsvoltmeter} (ECCV 2022) introduces physics-based stochastic process modeling of DVS pixel circuits. The choice of simulator significantly impacts the sim-to-real gap; recent work increasingly favors v2e and DVS-Voltmeter for their more realistic noise models.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:data_discussion}

Despite rapid growth, the event camera dataset ecosystem faces several structural challenges:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Scale gap.} The largest event classification dataset (N-ImageNet, 1.78M samples) is two orders of magnitude smaller than ImageNet (14M) and four orders smaller than web-scale datasets (\eg, LAION-5B). This limits the scale of pre-training and the diversity of learned representations.

    \item \textbf{Sensor bias.} The majority of datasets use DAVIS346 (346$\times$260) or Prophesee Gen3 (640$\times$480) sensors. Very few leverage the newer megapixel-class sensors (Gen4, EVK4-HD), limiting evaluation of methods at resolutions relevant to practical deployment.

    \item \textbf{Format fragmentation.} Event data is stored in diverse formats (\texttt{.aedat}, \texttt{.raw}, \texttt{.h5}, \texttt{.npy}, rosbag) with no community-wide standard, creating unnecessary friction for researchers.

    \item \textbf{No unified multi-task benchmark.} Unlike COCO (detection + segmentation + captioning) or KITTI (detection + depth + flow + SLAM), no single event dataset supports evaluation across all major tasks.

    \item \textbf{Language data scarcity.} The largest event-language dataset (EventMind, 500K+) is tiny compared to image-language scale (LAION-5B), constraining the development of event MLLMs.
\end{itemize}
