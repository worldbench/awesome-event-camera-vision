\begin{abstract}
Event cameras are bio-inspired neuromorphic sensors that asynchronously capture per-pixel brightness changes with microsecond temporal resolution, high dynamic range, and low power consumption. This survey reviews event camera vision in the era of large models, spanning perception, reconstruction, understanding, applications, datasets, and other resources. We organize recent advances with particular attention to vision transformers, diffusion models, state-space models, neural radiance fields, 3D Gaussian Splatting, and multimodal foundation models. For reconstruction, we distinguish dedicated event reconstruction paradigms from Foundation-Prior methods that materially leverage large-scale pretrained appearance, semantic, video, or geometric knowledge in their reconstruction or broader experimental pipeline, while retaining input modality, task, and 3D representation as explicit attributes. We further summarize representative datasets and benchmarks and identify open challenges for robust, efficient, and broadly deployable event-based vision. A continuously updated companion repository is provided to support the community.
\end{abstract}
